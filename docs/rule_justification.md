Generated by `python scripts/rule_analysis.py` on the cleaned **training** split (n = 6,892). "Approval rate" is the historical label. In the primary dataset the label comes from a latent repayment-risk score that does not use the rules (docs/data_decision.md), so a rule that the label ignores (e.g. age at maturity) shows little difference either side of its cutoff. Such rules rest on policy and regulation, not on the label, and the tables show their cost: how many applicants each one rejects or routes to review.

## Rationale and source for each rule

### R01 Minimum age (HARD)

- Condition: `age < 21`
- Rationale: Applicants must be able to enter a binding contract and show an income history. 21 is the common entry age for unsecured retail lending in Indian banks; 18 is the legal minimum, so 21 is a policy choice.
- Source: Bank credit policy (typical Indian retail practice); Indian Contract Act 1872 s.11 for the legal floor.

### R02 Fraud / blacklist registry hit (HARD)

- Condition: `blacklist_hit == true`
- Rationale: Lending to a known fraudster or wilful defaulter is prohibited regardless of score. The registry is a file (config/fraud_registry.yaml) so it can be updated without a code change. Not evaluated when the application has no Applicant_ID.
- Source: RBI Master Direction on Wilful Defaulters and Large Defaulters (2024); RBI Master Direction on Frauds.

### R03 Age at loan maturity (HARD)

- Condition: `age_at_maturity > 65`
- Rationale: Repayment must finish while the applicant still has earning capacity. A single cap of 65 replaces the earlier 60 (salaried) / 65 (self-employed) split; the data analysis in docs/rule_justification.md shows how many applicants each cutoff rejects. Flagged for tuning in the October plan.
- Source: Bank credit policy (typical home-loan maximum age at maturity 60-70 in Indian banks).

### R04 Minimum bureau score (HARD)

- Condition: `cibil_score < 650 unless new_to_credit`
- Rationale: Scores below 650 indicate recent delinquency. New-to-credit applicants (CIBIL -1) have no score, are exempt here and are routed to review by S01 instead of being rejected for lack of history.
- Source: Bank credit policy (650 is a widely used cut-off; TransUnion CIBIL score bands).

### R05 FOIR limit by income band (HARD)

- Condition: `foir > foir_limit`
- Rationale: Fixed obligations (existing EMIs plus the proposed EMI) must leave enough income to live on. Higher incomes can carry a larger share, hence the slabs 50% / 55% / 65%.
- Source: Bank credit policy (FOIR 50-65% is standard Indian underwriting practice).

### R06 RBI LTV cap for housing loans (HARD)

- Condition: `ltv > ltv_cap when loan_type == Home`
- Rationale: Regulatory ceiling on loan-to-value for housing loans: 90% up to Rs 30 lakh, 80% for Rs 30-75 lakh, 75% above Rs 75 lakh.
- Source: RBI Master Circular - Housing Finance (LTV ceilings for individual housing loans).

### R07 Minimum monthly income (HARD)

- Condition: `total_income < 15000`
- Rationale: Below this income even a small EMI leaves too little for living costs. Co-applicant income is clubbed.
- Source: Bank credit policy (typical minimum income for retail loans).

### S01 New to credit (SOFT)

- Condition: `new_to_credit == true`
- Rationale: Rejecting applicants only because they have no history would exclude first-time borrowers. A person reviews alternative evidence instead.
- Source: RBI financial-inclusion objectives; bank credit policy.

### S02 Work experience below minimum (SOFT)

- Condition: `work_experience < min_experience`
- Rationale: Short employment or business vintage raises income-continuity risk but is not disqualifying (job changes, new professionals). Salaried 1 year; self-employed 3 years of ITR.
- Source: Bank credit policy (ITR vintage norms for self-employed borrowers).

### S03 Borderline FOIR (SOFT)

- Condition: `foir_borderline == true`
- Rationale: Applicants just under the FOIR limit are one rate rise or one new obligation away from breaching it. A person confirms the obligations before approval.
- Source: Bank credit policy.

### S04 Missing key information (SOFT)

- Condition: `missing_key_data == true`
- Rationale: Optional fields that the rules or model rely on (bureau score, existing EMIs, experience, collateral value for a secured loan) are never guessed for a live decision; the case goes to a person.
- Source: RBI Fair Practices Code (lenders must assess applications on complete information).

## R03 decision

The data does **not** support R03: applicants it rejects were historically approved at 0.705, versus 0.584 for those it passes, because repayment risk in this dataset does not depend on age (older applicants have more experience, which the label rewards). R03 is therefore a policy constraint (no repayment after typical retirement), not a learned one, and its cost is the approvable applicants it turns away. The sweep shows that cost falls from 331 rejections at 60 to 112 at 65 and 38 at 70. v1 keeps a single cap of 65, the middle of the 60-70 range used by Indian lenders: it rejects about a third as many applicants as a 60 cap and removes the salaried/self-employed split. Recommendation for the October tuning: confirm 65 with the guide, or turn R03 into a SOFT rule (review instead of reject) for maturity ages 65-70.
